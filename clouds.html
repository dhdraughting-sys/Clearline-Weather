High cloud

Cirrus Ci
5,000-13,000 m · Ice crystals
Look for: Thin, wispy white streaks or filaments, sometimes hooked at one end into “mares’ tails” by strong high-altitude wind.
Often means: Usually fair weather right now, but thickening, spreading cirrus is often the first sign a warm front - and rain - is on the way in the next day or two.
Cirrocumulus Cc
5,000-13,000 m · Ice crystals (sometimes supercooled water)
Look for: Small white ripples or tufts in orderly rows, giving a dappled “mackerel sky”.
Often means: Fair for now, but a classic sign of unsettled, changeable weather moving in.
Cirrostratus Cs
5,000-13,000 m · Ice crystals
Look for: A thin, milky veil covering most or all of the sky - often only obvious because it puts a halo ring around the sun or moon.
Often means: Rain or snow within 12-24 hours is a common follow-up once this thickens.

Middle cloud

Altocumulus Ac
2,000-7,000 m · Mostly water droplets
Look for: Grey-white patches or rolls in clusters or lines - the classic “flock of sheep” sky.
Often means: Generally settled, but altocumulus on a warm, humid morning can signal thunderstorms building later that same day.
Altostratus As
2,000-7,000 m · Water droplets and ice crystals
Look for: A featureless grey or blue-grey sheet across the whole sky - the sun looks like it's behind frosted glass, no halo, no sharp shadows.
Often means: Usually the lead-in to a spell of continuous rain or snow, thickening from here into nimbostratus.
Nimbostratus Ns
Surface-3,000 m (thick, deep layer) · Water droplets and ice crystals
Look for: A thick, dark grey, featureless blanket that blots out the sun completely.
Often means: Steady, prolonged rain or snow - the least dramatic-looking cloud, but the one that actually soaks you all day.

Low cloud

Stratocumulus Sc
Surface-2,000 m · Water droplets
Look for: Low, lumpy grey-and-white patches or rolls, usually with some blue sky breaking through between them.
Often means: The everyday “bit cloudy” sky - generally dry, maybe the odd spot of light drizzle.
Stratus St
Surface-2,000 m · Water droplets
Look for: A flat, featureless grey layer, often low enough to sit on hilltops as mist or fog.
Often means: Drizzle, fine mist, or murky, dull conditions rather than heavy rain.
Cumulus Cu
Surface-2,000 m base (can build much higher) · Water droplets
Look for: Puffy, cotton-wool clouds with a flat base and a bulging, sunlit top - the classic “fair weather” cloud drawn in every kid's sky.
Often means: Fine weather while small. If they keep growing taller through the day (“cumulus congestus”), showers or storms can follow by afternoon.

Vertical cloud

Cumulonimbus Cb
Surface up to 12,000 m+ (a single towering cloud) · Water droplets low down, ice at the top
Look for: A huge, dark-based tower, often flattening into a wide anvil shape at the top where it hits the upper atmosphere.
Often means: Thunderstorms, heavy rain, hail, and lightning - the one cloud worth taking seriously.
